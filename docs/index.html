The 102nd member takes the chair

A complete simulated fruit-fly brain, all 166,700 neurons of it, sits in the Speaker's seat of the Estonian parliament while a real sitting is played to it. Every speech reaches it as a scent from the speaker's side of the hall. Every insult comes straight at it. When its escape reflex fires, it rings the bell.

Its memory lasts 20 milliseconds. It hears the hundredth Aitäh! as the first, and every heckler is a stranger. The page keeps the tally the brain cannot, and shows what the real chair did at the same moments.

How it works

the hallThe Riigikogu's own transcript, second by second: who spoke, from which seat, what was heckled, when the chair rang the bell. Seating comes from the hall plan.
the brainThe MaleCNS connectome, every neuron and synapse of one male fruit fly, run as a spiking model. A speech is a scent on one side; a hostile speech or a heckle is an object rushing at the fly. The giant fibre, its escape reflex, decides the bell.
the honestyThe tone of each speech is judged by a language model with one calibrated question, not by the fly. What the fly adds is the reflex itself: its 4 ms timing, its side, and the damping that lets a merely sharp speech pass while a heckle never does.

The fly is the same instrument on both days: a heckle always rings, a civil speech almost never, reaction time 4 ms. The human chair is a different instrument. It polices the room; the fly polices the speaker.

A simulation: a real fly's wiring, with inputs and outputs of our choosing. It makes no claim about what a fly experiences.
